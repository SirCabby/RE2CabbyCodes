# RE2CabbyCodes

A cheat panel for **Resident Evil 2** (the 2019 remake) on Steam. Pause the game (Esc, or Start on a
pad) and a small panel appears beside the pause menu:

- **God mode** - every hit you take is healed as it lands, and poison is cured at once.
- **One hit kills** - any hit that does damage kills the enemy (deaths and drops still play out
  through the game's own code). Mr. X cannot be killed: the first hit puts him down on his knees, as
  running his health out does, and he gets up again as usual. The plants normally have to be shot in
  the glands to drop and then burned to finish; here any hit that does damage burns one up on the
  spot - no glands to find, no fire to bring, and no knocking it down first. An enemy in a scripted
  moment (a zombie climbing in through a window, a licker's entrance) dies as soon as the moment ends.
  Enemies the game holds at their last point of health - Mr. X on a ladder, or while you break free of
  his grab - are left to it.
- **Infinite ammo** - firing uses no ammo: the gun in your hands keeps its loaded rounds, the
  flamethrower and the spark shot their charge. Pickups and reloads work as usual; knives and grenades
  are left alone.
- **No durability loss** - knives never wear down: the knife you hold is kept full through every hit,
  so it never breaks (a worn one is made full when the cheat goes on).
- **Infinite wooden boards** - boarding up a window uses no Wooden Boards: the window is boarded as
  usual and your boards stay in the inventory (you still need to carry some). Nothing else is kept.
- **Save without ink ribbons** - on Hardcore, typewriters save without asking for an ink ribbon: the
  typewriter goes straight to the save menu, as it does on Standard. A ribbon you are carrying is still
  used up by the save, as the game's save menu takes one on Hardcore.
- **Save without counting** - saves add nothing to the save count the results screen ranks you on:
  each save records the count it had when the cheat went on.
- **Save count** and **play time** editors - change the counter and the time (hours, minutes,
  seconds) your saves record and the results screen ranks you on. The play time you set is what the
  game records from then on, and it keeps counting from there.
- **Freeze play time** - the time your saves and your clear record stops where it is, in the main game
  and in The Ghost Survivors, The 4th Survivor and The Tofu Survivor (whose mission times are kept the
  moment the run ends). The Ghost Survivors' run timer on screen stands still with it. The game's own
  clock keeps running underneath: the enemies' timers are on it, and stopping it leaves them standing
  about and can stall a scripted scene until the game is restarted - which is why the pause menu's own
  play time keeps counting even with this on. The panel's figure is the one that will be recorded.
- **Freeze countdown timer** - the self-destruct countdown stops where it is; its alarms and your
  escape still work as usual.
- **Item box without counting** - opening the item box is not counted, for the record for finishing
  the game without opening it: the game's count goes to 0 when the cheat goes on and stays there while
  it is on, in your saves too. Turned off, openings count again from there. The panel shows the count.
- **Recovery items without counting** - the same for the record for finishing without using a
  recovery item (a first aid spray, a herb or a mix): the count goes to 0 and stays there. The item
  still heals you and is used up.
- **Step count** editor and **Freeze step count** - the steps the game counts for the record for
  finishing in 14000 steps or fewer: set the count, and freeze it so the steps you take are not
  counted (in your saves too).
- **Difficulty** - switch the game you are playing between Assisted, Standard and Hardcore on the
  spot, with nothing reloaded. It is the game's own switch, the one a new game and every load make, so
  what the game checks as it goes follows at once: Assisted's aim assist and slow healing, the
  typewriters' ink ribbons on Hardcore, and the game's rank, held to the new difficulty's range from its
  next change. What the game set up when it loaded the area you are in stays as it is until that area
  loads again. Your next save records the new
  difficulty; loading a save made before the switch brings that save's difficulty back. A clear
  counts for the difficulty you finish on.
- **Inventory editor** - every slot of the character you are playing: pick any item **or weapon** for a
  slot and set its count, or empty it. Open a slot's list and type to narrow it (by name, the name an
  item has before it is examined, or its number); Enter takes the first match. Item names are the game's
  own; where two items read exactly the same, the later one shows its internal code too
  (`Green Herb (sm70_051)`). The weapons are listed under the items - the guns, the knives and the
  Hand and Flash Grenades, which the game counts as weapons rather than items - and one arrives with no
  parts fitted, full of the ammo the game says it takes; the count beside a gun is its loaded rounds, and
  beside grenades how many you carry. A weapon goes in and comes out through the game's own inventory
  functions, so it is armed and put away properly: that needs those functions and one empty entry in the
  item box for the game to be handed the weapon in, and the list says so when either is missing. The
  infinite weapons are the game's rewards - unless the record that gives one is earned (the records panel),
  the game takes it back out of your inventory at the next load. Items that take two slots still come in
  through the item box below.
- **Item box** - the game's own item box, reachable from the pause menu, with no limits on what moves:
  **Take** puts a box item into the first free inventory slot (a two-slot item into the first two free
  slots side by side - weapons included that grow to two slots with a part fitted, like the Matilda
  with its stock), **x** throws one away, and **Store** beside an inventory slot puts whatever it
  holds - weapons included - into the box. Take and Store run the game's own inventory code, the same
  its item boxes use, so a weapon is armed when it comes out and unequipped and put away when it goes
  in. Store stacks the way the game's box does: ammo and the other items the game stacks top up the
  stacks of the same item already in the box, up to the game's own maximum per stack (60 handgun
  rounds, 20 shotgun shells, 200 submachine gun rounds, ...), and the rest becomes a new stack - or,
  when the box has no empty entry left, stays in the slot. Take moves a stack whole. The list is filed
  under five tabs (key items, weapons, ammo, heals, other), sorted by name, with a filter box.

### Records

Open either of the game's records screens - **Records** or **Records (The Ghost Survivors)**, from the
title's Bonus menu or the pause menu - and the panel shows that screen's records instead of the cheats,
each with a switch, what it gives, and its progress:

- **Switch a record on** and it is complete, as if you had earned it: its rewards unlock - game modes
  (the 2nd runs, The 4th Survivor, The Tofu Survivor and its other characters), costumes, the infinite
  weapons, concept art and figures; in The Ghost Survivors, its accessory - and what earns it is set the
  way the game sets it. A record marked **[achievement]** unlocks its Steam achievement too (41 of the
  main game's records and 2 of The Ghost Survivors' have one). Steam keeps an achievement for good:
  switching the record off again does not take it back.
- **Switch a record off** and it is incomplete again: its rewards are locked (unless another complete
  record gives the same one), and what earned it is taken back just far enough for you to earn it again
  by playing - a kill count drops just short of its goal, one Mr. Raccoon can be broken again, the Ghost
  Survivors' play count drops just short, a special condition is forgotten. A Ghost Survivors
  accessory is taken off whoever wears it (Cat Ears no longer gives infinite ammo). "Master of
  Unlocking" makes one safe or lock you opened count as unopened again. Records that count things found once -
  files, film, weapons and parts - stay off until you switch them on here: the game remembers each
  find, so finding them again does not count.
- **Nothing is locked away.** No Way Out opens once Forgotten Soldier, Runaway and No Time To Mourn
  are all cleared, and the game keeps no flag of its own for it - so while it is open, switching off
  "Mission Complete?", "Reunited" or "Getting Over It" does not un-clear the scenario: the record is
  **held off** by the mod instead (clearing the scenario again will not bring it back; switch it on in
  the panel). The no-training records keep their scenario cleared, as a training clear.
- **Complete all** and **Clear all** switch the whole screen's set (with a confirmation first), and a
  filter box narrows the list. The main game's four hidden records (the infinite ATM-4 and Minigun, and
  The Tofu Survivor's other characters) are listed after the rest.

Every change is saved at once - the mod asks the game for the same save it makes after a typewriter
save. The game's list shows a change once you move its cursor. The menus and the costume, figure and
concept art screens follow the next time they open; an infinite weapon arrives in the item box, or
leaves the item box and your inventory, the next time a game is loaded. A costume you are wearing stays
on until you change it. "Raccoon City Native" (every record complete) is given back by the game itself
whenever all the others are complete. The arrow in the window's title bar (or a double-click on the
title) folds it away, to see the game's screen behind it; it stays folded until you open it again.

### Save files

Open the game's **Load Game** screen - from the title menu, the pause menu or the game over screen -
and the panel lists the game's own save slots beside it, the auto-save and slots 1-20, each with what
the game's list says of it (scenario, difficulty, where and when it was saved):

- **Delete** removes a save. The game deletes it itself - the delete its save manager has for this,
  which the game's own menus never offer - and the slot reads No Data from then on; your next save can
  go there.
- **Copy to...** puts a copy of a save into another slot, empty or not (a save already there is
  replaced). The copy is written where the game keeps its saves - Steam Cloud, through Steam's own
  storage functions, as the game saves - read back to check it, and loads like the original. Copy a
  save to keep it before a risky choice, or copy the auto-save into a slot before it moves on.

Both ask first and change your saves straight away, so neither can be undone. The game's list beside
the panel is read again after each change (its cursor goes back to the last save you loaded). The
system save - records, rewards, options - and The Ghost Survivors' save are not in the list and are
never touched.

Tick or edit what you want and unpause. The switches keep their state for the session (start-up
defaults live in the ini). Click the **Cheats** header to fold the cheats away when you are sorting
the inventory: the item box list grows into the room it leaves. **F7** hides or shows the panel on the pause menu; anywhere else it opens
the panel over the game (and says what the mod is still waiting for, if anything), and F7 closes it.

Works on Windows and on Linux/Proton with no launch options. It leaves `dinput8.dll` alone, so it
can sit beside REFramework.

## Install

1. Open the game's folder (in Steam: right-click the game, *Manage*, *Browse local files*) - the one
   containing `re2.exe`.
2. Rename the existing `steam_api64.dll` to `steam_api64_orig.dll`.
3. Copy the mod's `steam_api64.dll` in beside it.

Steam's *Verify integrity of game files* puts the stock DLL back; just repeat step 3 if that happens.
To uninstall, delete the mod's `steam_api64.dll` and rename `steam_api64_orig.dll` back.

If the game stops with *steam_api64_orig.dll is missing or broken*, step 2 was skipped: verify the
game files in Steam (that brings the stock DLL back), then do steps 2 and 3 again.

## Please read before using

- This is a first version, built from the game's own data and tested outside the game; the first
  sessions with it are its real test. Back up your saves first:
  `Steam/userdata/<your id>/883710/remote/win64_save/`.
- The cheats change what the results screen ranks you on (clear time, saves) and the records it
  awards (item box, recovery items, steps), and a save made while cheating records the game as it was.
- God mode heals every hit, but an attack that kills outright - a blow worth more than all your health,
  or being eaten - still does.
- One hit kills changes boss fights: a boss whose fight is scripted around its health may end sooner. A
  few special hits (G2 clinging on, some breakable enemy parts) still do their normal damage.
- The inventory and the item box are the game's own: a change is kept by your next save and lost, like
  anything else, if you quit without saving - an item thrown away is gone for good once you save. They
  change only while the pause menu is up, and a change the mod cannot make safely is refused, with the
  reason in the panel.
- The records panel changes your system save - the records, rewards and Ghost Survivors progress every
  save slot shares - and saves it at once. Back it up before trying it (the whole `win64_save` folder).
- The save files' Delete and Copy change your saves (and Steam Cloud) at once and cannot be undone:
  back up the `win64_save` folder before trying them.
- Only the current Steam build (11636119, June 2023) has been looked at. Everything the mod uses is
  found by name in the game's own type information, so in a build where something is missing, that
  feature is greyed out with the reason beside it, and the log says what was missing.

## Config

`RE2CabbyCodes.ini` is created next to the DLL on first run:

| Key | Default | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `ToggleKey` | `0x76` (F7) | virtual-key code that hides/shows the panel |
| `GodMode`, `OneHitKills`, `InfiniteAmmo`, `NoDurabilityLoss`, `InfiniteWoodenBoards`, `SaveWithoutInkRibbons`, `NoSaveCount`, `FreezePlaytime`, `FreezeCountdown`, `NoItemBoxCount`, `NoRecoveryItemCount`, `FreezeSteps` | `0` | cheats switched on when the game starts (`InfiniteInkRibbons`, the old name, is still read) |
| `AlwaysShow` | `0` | debug: draw the panel outside the pause menu too |
| `Trace` | `0` | verbose diagnostics in `RE2CabbyCodes.log` |
| `DumpMethods` | `0` | write every game method's code address beside the DLL once (for reverse engineering) |
| `Disable` | | comma list of subsystems to turn off: `overlay,dispatch,game,events,input,cursor,keyboard` |

`RE2CabbyCodes.log` beside the DLL records what the mod found and did, and the run before it is kept
as `RE2CabbyCodes.prev.log` - attach both when reporting a problem. `RE2CabbyCodes.imgui.ini`
remembers where you left the panel and whether its cheats are folded away. `RE2CabbyCodes.records.txt`
is written by the records panel: the Ghost Survivors records it holds off (see Records).

## How it works

The mod ships as a proxy `steam_api64.dll` in front of the game's own (renamed
`steam_api64_orig.dll`): every export is forwarded untouched through generated jump thunks, so it
loads with the game and needs no injector. `re2.exe` is never modified. RE Engine carries a complete
description of its own classes - the type database its managed runtime runs on - and the mod finds
everything through it by name: the game's managers (the flow, the players, the enemies, the records,
the clock, the inventory, the item box), the fields it reads and writes, and the values of the game's
enumerations. Nothing is a fixed address or a hard-coded offset. The cheats act only when the game
does: the game calls the mod at its own events - an enemy or the player hit, a shot, a knife's hit, a
typewriter's check, boards picked for a window, a save, the clock's frame, the item box opening, the
inventory screen's frame, a footstep - because the mod points the game's own tables (the
entries delegates are made from, the vtables of a few classes) at small functions that do the cheat's
work around the game's original code. Nothing is read while you play but where the game is (one value a
frame), and no code is patched. The inventory editor changes the objects the game already has with
checked writes. The item box's Take and Store, the weapons the inventory editor puts in and takes out, and
the difficulty switch are the only places the mod calls the game, with the records panel's Steam
achievements: those all run the game's own inventory functions, because only those arm and
disarm a weapon, the difficulty switch runs the game's own, because it also sets flags the engine
keeps outside the game's objects, and a record switched on unlocks its achievement through the game's
own achievement manager (found by name, called the way the engine calls them, and switched off for the
session if one ever fails). The records panel knows a records screen is up from that screen's own
frame (its update, hooked like the cheats' events), and the save files the Load Game screen alike: a
save is deleted by the game's own save manager, a copy is written through Steam's storage functions -
the ones the game keeps its saves with - and then the game is asked to read its list of saves again
(two of its own functions). The editors change the game only while the pause menu has it stopped,
from inside the game's own frame update; the cheats' events run where the game calls them. Item and weapon names come from the game's own text in its archives. The panel is
Dear ImGui, drawn into the game's swap chain through Direct3D 11 or 12, whichever the game uses. It
reads the keyboard through a DirectInput keyboard of its own - the game can take the keyboard for
itself, which leaves its window no key messages to type with - and while you type into the panel the
game's own keyboard is told no key went down (Escape still reaches the pause menu). See
`CLAUDE.md` for the reverse-engineering record.

The type database's layout comes from praydog's [REFramework](https://github.com/praydog/REFramework)
(MIT), whose SDK documents it.

## Building from source

Linux with mingw-w64 (`x86_64-w64-mingw32-g++`); no Windows or MSVC needed.

```sh
cp config.mk.example config.mk   # set GAME_DIR (the folder with re2.exe)
make                             # build/steam_api64.dll
make install                     # deploy into GAME_DIR (renames the stock DLL once)
make uninstall                   # put the stock DLL back
make version 1.1.0               # set the version (VERSION file, baked into the DLL)
make package                     # dist/RE2CabbyCodes_v<version>.zip
make proxy                       # regenerate the export list from the stock DLL
```

`tools/` holds the reverse-engineering helpers - reading the type database and the game's text out of
the files on disk, generating the item table, the records panel's names and the proxy's export list -
described in `CLAUDE.md`.
