[size=6][b]RE2CabbyCodes[/b][/size]

A [b]cheat panel[/b] for Resident Evil 2 (the 2019 remake): pause the game and tick what you want - God mode, One hit kills, Infinite ammo, No durability loss, Infinite wooden boards, Save without ink ribbons, Save without counting, editors and freezes for the save count, the play time, the self-destruct countdown and the step count, a difficulty switch, an inventory editor and the game's own item box. Beside the game's records screens it switches any record - and its rewards - on or off, and beside Load Game it deletes a save file or copies one into another slot.

This mod is open source! Check it out at [url=https://github.com/SirCabby/RE2CabbyCodes]https://github.com/SirCabby/RE2CabbyCodes[/url]

[size=5][b]What it does[/b][/size]
[list]
[*][b]God mode[/b] - every hit you take is healed as it lands, and poison is cured at once.
[*][b]One hit kills[/b] - any hit that does damage kills the enemy (deaths and drops still play out through the game's own code). Mr. X cannot be killed: the first hit puts him on his knees, as running his health out does, and he gets up again as usual. The plants burn up on any hit - no glands to shoot, no fire to bring, no knocking one down first. An enemy in a scripted moment (a zombie climbing in through a window) dies as soon as the moment ends, and enemies the game holds at their last point of health are left to it.
[*][b]Infinite ammo[/b] - firing uses no ammo: the gun in your hands keeps its loaded rounds, the flamethrower and the spark shot their charge. Pickups and reloads work as usual; knives and grenades are left alone.
[*][b]No durability loss[/b] - the knife you hold is kept full through every hit, so it never breaks (a worn one is made full when the cheat goes on).
[*][b]Infinite wooden boards[/b] - boarding up a window uses no Wooden Boards: the window is boarded as usual and your boards stay in the inventory (you still need to carry some).
[*][b]Save without ink ribbons[/b] - on Hardcore, typewriters go straight to the save menu instead of asking for an ink ribbon, as they do on Standard. A ribbon you are carrying is still used up by the save, as the game's save menu takes one on Hardcore.
[*][b]Save without counting[/b] - saves add nothing to the save count the results screen ranks you on: each save records the count it had when the cheat went on.
[*][b]Save count[/b], [b]play time[/b] and [b]step count[/b] editors - change the counter, the clock and the steps your saves record and the results screen ranks you on. The play time you set is what the game records from then on, and it keeps counting from there.
[*][b]Freeze play time[/b] - the time your saves and your clear record stops where it is, in the main game and in The Ghost Survivors, The 4th Survivor and The Tofu Survivor (whose mission times are kept the moment the run ends). The Ghost Survivors' run timer on screen stands still with it. The game's own clock keeps running underneath - the enemies' timers are on it, and stopping it would leave them standing about - which is why the pause menu's own play time keeps counting even with this on. The panel's figure is the one that will be recorded.
[*][b]Freeze countdown timer[/b] - the self-destruct countdown stops where it is; its alarms and your escape still work as usual.
[*][b]Item box without counting[/b], [b]Recovery items without counting[/b] and [b]Freeze step count[/b] - the three records for finishing without opening the item box, without using a recovery item, and in 14000 steps or fewer: the count goes to 0, or holds where it is, and stays there while the switch is on, in your saves too. Turned off, it counts again from there. A recovery item still heals you and is still used up. The panel shows all three counts.
[*][b]Difficulty[/b] - switch the game you are playing between Assisted, Standard and Hardcore on the spot, with nothing reloaded. It is the game's own switch, the one a new game and every load make, so what the game checks as it goes follows at once: Assisted's aim assist and slow healing, the typewriters' ink ribbons on Hardcore, and the game's rank. What the game set up when it loaded the area you are in stays as it is until that area loads again. Your next save records the new difficulty, loading an older save brings that save's difficulty back, and a clear counts for the difficulty you finish on.
[*][b]Inventory editor[/b] - every slot of the character you are playing: pick any item [b]or weapon[/b] for a slot and set its count, or empty it. Type to narrow a slot's list (by name, the name an item has before it is examined, or its number); Enter takes the first match. The weapons are listed under the items - the guns, the knives and the Hand and Flash Grenades, which the game counts as weapons rather than items - and one arrives with no parts fitted, full of the ammo the game says it takes. A weapon goes in and comes out through the game's own inventory functions, so it is armed and put away properly. The infinite weapons are the game's rewards: unless the record that gives one is earned, the game takes it back out at the next load.
[*][b]Item box[/b] - the game's own item box, reachable from the pause menu, with no limits on what moves: [b]Take[/b] puts a box item into the first free inventory slot (a two-slot item into the first two free slots side by side - weapons included that grow to two slots with a part fitted, like the Matilda with its stock), [b]x[/b] throws one away, and [b]Store[/b] beside an inventory slot puts whatever it holds - weapons included - into the box. Take and Store run the game's own inventory code, the same its item boxes use. Store stacks the way the game's box does, up to the game's own maximum per stack (60 handgun rounds, 20 shotgun shells, 200 submachine gun rounds, ...), and what fits nowhere stays in the slot; Take moves a stack whole. The list is filed under five tabs - key items, weapons, ammo, heals, other - sorted by name, with a filter box.
[*][b]F7[/b] hides or shows the panel on the pause menu; anywhere else it opens the panel over the game, and F7 closes it again. Start-up defaults live in the ini.
[/list]

[size=4][b]Records[/b][/size]

Open either of the game's records screens - [b]Records[/b] or [b]Records (The Ghost Survivors)[/b], from the title's Bonus menu or the pause menu - and the panel shows that screen's records instead of the cheats, each with a switch, what it gives, and its progress:
[list]
[*][b]Switch a record on[/b] and it is complete, as if you had earned it: its rewards unlock - game modes (the 2nd runs, The 4th Survivor, The Tofu Survivor and its other characters), costumes, the infinite weapons, concept art and figures; in The Ghost Survivors, its accessory. A record the panel marks as carrying a [b]Steam achievement[/b] unlocks that too (41 of the main game's records and 2 of The Ghost Survivors' have one). [b]Steam keeps an achievement for good[/b] - switching the record off again does not take it back.
[*][b]Switch a record off[/b] and it is incomplete again: its rewards are locked (unless another complete record gives the same one), and what earned it is taken back just far enough for you to earn it again by playing - a kill count drops just short of its goal, one Mr. Raccoon can be broken again, a special condition is forgotten. A Ghost Survivors accessory is taken off whoever wears it (Cat Ears no longer gives infinite ammo). Records that count things found once - files, film, weapons and parts - stay off until you switch them on here: the game remembers each find, so finding them again does not count.
[*][b]Nothing is locked away.[/b] No Way Out opens once Forgotten Soldier, Runaway and No Time To Mourn are all cleared, and the game keeps no flag of its own for it - so while it is open, switching off "Mission Complete?", "Reunited" or "Getting Over It" does not un-clear the scenario: the record is [b]held off[/b] by the mod instead (clearing the scenario again will not bring it back; switch it on in the panel).
[*][b]Complete all[/b] and [b]Clear all[/b] switch the whole screen's set, with a confirmation first, and a filter box narrows the list. The main game's four hidden records (the infinite ATM-4 and Minigun, and The Tofu Survivor's other characters) are listed after the rest.
[/list]
Every change is saved at once - the mod asks the game for the same save it makes after a typewriter save. The game's list shows a change once you move its cursor. The menus and the costume, figure and concept art screens follow the next time they open; an infinite weapon arrives in the item box, or leaves the item box and your inventory, the next time a game is loaded. "Raccoon City Native" (every record complete) is given back by the game itself whenever all the others are complete.

[size=4][b]Save files[/b][/size]

Open the game's [b]Load Game[/b] screen - from the title menu, the pause menu or the game over screen - and the panel lists the game's own save slots beside it, the auto-save and slots 1-20, each with what the game's list says of it (scenario, difficulty, where and when it was saved):
[list]
[*][b]Delete[/b] removes a save. The game deletes it itself - the delete its save manager has for this, which the game's own menus never offer - and the slot reads No Data from then on; your next save can go there.
[*][b]Copy to...[/b] puts a copy of a save into another slot, empty or not (a save already there is replaced). The copy is written where the game keeps its saves - Steam Cloud, through Steam's own storage functions, as the game saves - read back to check it, and loads like the original. Copy a save to keep it before a risky choice, or copy the auto-save into a slot before it moves on.
[/list]
Both ask first and change your saves straight away, so neither can be undone. The game's list beside the panel is read again after each change. The system save - records, rewards, options - and The Ghost Survivors' save are not in the list and are never touched.

[size=5][b]Install[/b][/size]

Copy into the game's folder - the one with re2.exe (in Steam: right-click the game > Manage > Browse local files):
[list=1]
[*]Rename the stock [b]steam_api64.dll[/b] to [b]steam_api64_orig.dll[/b]
[*]Drop this mod's [b]steam_api64.dll[/b] in its place
[/list]

That is the whole install, on [b]Windows and Linux/Proton alike[/b] - no launch options, no WINEDLLOVERRIDES. It leaves dinput8.dll alone, so it can sit beside REFramework.

To uninstall: delete steam_api64.dll and rename steam_api64_orig.dll back.

[b]Steam's "Verify integrity of game files" removes the mod[/b] by restoring the stock DLL. Just re-copy the file. If the game stops with [b]steam_api64_orig.dll is missing or broken[/b], the rename was skipped: verify the game files in Steam (that brings the stock DLL back), then do both steps again.

[size=5][b]Please read before using[/b][/size]
[list]
[*][b]This is a first version[/b], built from the game's own data and tested outside the game; the first sessions with it are its real test. [b]Back up your saves first[/b]: Steam/userdata/<your id>/883710/remote/win64_save/.
[*][b]The records panel changes your system save[/b] - the records, rewards and Ghost Survivors progress every save slot shares - and saves it at once. Back up the whole win64_save folder before trying it.
[*][b]The save files' Delete and Copy change your saves (and Steam Cloud) at once and cannot be undone[/b] - back up the win64_save folder before trying them.
[*]The cheats change what the results screen ranks you on (clear time, saves) and the records it awards (item box, recovery items, steps), and a save made while cheating records the game as it was.
[*]God mode heals every hit, but an attack that kills outright - a blow worth more than all your health, or being eaten - still does.
[*]One hit kills changes boss fights: a boss whose fight is scripted around its health may end sooner. A few special hits (G2 clinging on, some breakable enemy parts) still do their normal damage.
[*]The inventory and the item box are the game's own: a change is kept by your next save and lost, like anything else, if you quit without saving - an item thrown away is gone for good once you save. They change only while the pause menu is up, and a change the mod cannot make safely is refused, with the reason in the panel.
[*]Only the current Steam build (11636119, June 2023) has been looked at. Everything the mod uses is found by name in the game's own type information, so in a build where something is missing, that feature is greyed out with the reason beside it, and the log says what was missing.
[*]Tested on Linux/Proton.
[/list]

[size=5][b]Config[/b][/size]

[code]
ToggleKey             = 0x76  ; virtual-key code that hides/shows the panel (0x76 = F7)
GodMode               = 0     ; cheats switched on when the game starts
OneHitKills           = 0
InfiniteAmmo          = 0
NoDurabilityLoss      = 0     ; knives never wear down
InfiniteWoodenBoards  = 0
SaveWithoutInkRibbons = 0     ; Hardcore typewriters do not ask for a ribbon
NoSaveCount           = 0     ; save without adding to the save count
FreezePlaytime        = 0
FreezeCountdown       = 0     ; stop the self-destruct countdown
NoItemBoxCount        = 0     ; opening the item box is not counted
NoRecoveryItemCount   = 0     ; using a recovery item is not counted
FreezeSteps           = 0     ; steps are not counted
[/code]

Lives in [b]RE2CabbyCodes.ini[/b] next to re2.exe (created on first run). [b]RE2CabbyCodes.log[/b] beside it records what the mod found and did, and the run before it is kept as [b]RE2CabbyCodes.prev.log[/b] - attach both when reporting a problem. [b]RE2CabbyCodes.imgui.ini[/b] remembers where you left the panel, and [b]RE2CabbyCodes.records.txt[/b] holds the Ghost Survivors records the panel is keeping switched off. The ini takes a few diagnostic keys too (AlwaysShow, Trace, DumpMethods, Disable), described on the GitHub page.
